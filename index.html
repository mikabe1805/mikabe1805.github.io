01 / Applied AI research

Kuiper TTS

Screen readers are unreliable with math, which leaves most scientific papers closed to blind and low-vision readers. Kuiper turns a paper, equations included, into audio a listener can actually follow, and is built to run offline on a phone.

My contribution

I led the 18-person team: I helped recruit it, assigned work across the codebase, and ran the text-to-speech research and experiments behind the paper. I wrote the narration rules that make spoken equations easy to follow, so “d q over d t” becomes “the derivative of q with respect to t,” rebuilt the GPU training pipeline, and fine-tuned a science-reading voice on 200 sentences I recorded myself.

Co-author · 2nd Place Best Full Paper Award, IEEE ISEC 2026 ↗

02 / Life-RPG habit app · Solo developer

Room of Days

A life RPG for the small stuff. Chores and habits become Quests that earn XP, grow stats like Mind and Home, and furnish a warm room of your own. The idea is to make little moments count: you feel rewarded for what you just did and can watch it add up to something.

I started it on my own and designed and built every layer, from the art direction and music to the offline storage. It is on the App Store and has 10+ active users.

03 / Cross-device notebook · Solo developer

Room Notes

The notebook I wanted for engineering classes. Handwrite on an iPad, capture the board with your phone, type equations and code on a laptop, and all of it lands on the same page, laid out the same way everywhere. Most note apps treat the phone and laptop as smaller copies of the tablet app. Room Notes gives each device the job it does best.

I started it in August, build it solo, and take my own lecture notes in it.

04 / Interactive learning · Team project

Knowsy
Breadboard Lab

A breadboard in the browser for Rutgers’ Digital Logic and Principles of EE II labs. Students wire 16 real 74LS-series chips while a guided checklist confirms each step, and the simulator flags mistakes like a floating input or a shorted output.

I lead the module in a three-person team. I wrote the analog circuit solver from scratch in plain JavaScript, which drives the oscilloscope and Bode plotter, and the core of the logic engine.
