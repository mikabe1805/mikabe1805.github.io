About

I'm a Rutgers ECE student with a minor in CS, and I've been building things since middle school. Not because I had to, but because I realized early on that I could create experiences other people would actually use.

When I work on a project, I try to hold two perspectives at once: the user's and the manager's. On the user side, I'm asking whether it's intuitive, whether it's fun to come back to, whether it gets out of the way and just works. On the manager side, I'm making sure things are clean, reproducible, and maintainable. I don't stop iterating until both feel right.

My background spans hardware and software, from embedded systems and circuit design to full-stack development and ML research that got published at IEEE. I'm most drawn to problems where the two have to work closely together. The goal is always the same: build something that genuinely improves someone's life, and make it feel effortless to use.

Research & Publication
2nd Best Full Paper — IEEE ISEC 2026
Making Legacy Scientific Papers Accessible by Converting Equations into Spoken Language
16th IEEE Integrated STEM Education Conference · Princeton University · March 2026
K. August, D. Kruger, D. Lambropoulos, T. Willis III, J. Komitas, J. Li, V. Lawrence, A. Dhillon, Y. Kumar, S. Ghosh, M. Be

Peer-reviewed and presented at Princeton as a second-year student. The paper addresses a real gap in accessibility tooling for STEM: most scientific PDFs contain LaTeX-rendered equations that screen readers either skip or render as raw markup, making decades of published research inaccessible to visually impaired readers.

My contributions covered the full ML training infrastructure, rebuilding a broken stack from scratch and implementing the LaTeX-to-speech prosody layer that converts structural math notation into natural, listener-friendly narration.
