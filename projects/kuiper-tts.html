A voice needs something worth saying.

A scientific paper can look perfectly readable while its extracted text loses the equations. And even when the symbols survive, reading them one at a time can hide their meaning.

Kuiper TTS explores a more useful path: recover structure from the document, express the mathematics as language, and pass that language to a speech engine. The work focuses on access for blind and low-vision readers, with potential uses for anyone who benefits from listening.

My part in the work.

I led an 18-member interdisciplinary team through manuscript preparation and reproducibility work, co-authored the IEEE ISEC 2026 paper, and built speech and training tooling around the project. That included a Piper synthesis wrapper, dataset preparation and recording workflows, a testbench runner, and a reproducible GPU training and ONNX export pipeline. For the custom voice, I recorded 200 training sentences in my own voice.

In the equation-to-speech work, I built Python utilities that extract narration segments from LaTeX, rewrite mathematical expressions into speech-oriented text (so “d q over d t” becomes “the derivative of q with respect to t”), and write audio with output manifests. That makes the intermediate result visible before evaluating the voice.

Two related pieces of work: The conference paper describes the document-conversion and eSpeak/Piper narration system. My custom voice and training work is continued project development. The saved audio below is from that custom voice workflow.

Structure before synthesis.

The research pipeline renders a PDF page by page, uses a vision-language model to recover an intermediate LaTeX representation, and cleans and validates that output before preparing the narration.

Why preserve an intermediate representation?

It gives the system something people and code can inspect. A transcription error can be examined separately from a narration error, and the speech backend can change without redesigning the document-conversion stages.

Why process pages individually?

Long documents can exceed model context limits. Page-level conversion isolates a problematic page and lets rendering resolution change when equations or small text need more detail.

Why deterministic narration rules?

A derivative is an operation, not just letters separated by a bar. Explicit phrasing and grouping let the speech stage receive clearer language without depending on the voice model to infer the mathematics.

What the evidence tells us.

The paper reports that its Qwen-based OCR approach was more useful than the tested DeepSeek OCR approach, while still missing equations near page boundaries. That is an important failure case: successful text extraction does not establish that the mathematics survived.

The evaluation described in the paper is limited. OCR throughput and example outputs do not establish equation accuracy across all documents or improved comprehension for listeners. Equation accuracy and listener comprehension need their own evaluation, beyond a successful conversion or a clear-sounding recording.

Engineering lesson

Separate document fidelity, narration fidelity, and voice quality. A convincing voice can make an incorrect equation sound more authoritative; the intermediate text needs its own checks.
